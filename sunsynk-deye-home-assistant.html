How to connect a Sunsynk or Deye inverter
to Home Assistant.

There are three ways to get a Sunsynk or Deye hybrid inverter into Home Assistant: through the manufacturer's cloud, by building your own RS485 reader, or with a ready-made local gateway. This guide explains how each one works, what it costs you in time and freshness of data, and which one suits the automations you want to run.

Route 1

The cloud integration

Home Assistant has an official Sunsynk integration. It signs in with your Sunsynk Connect account and reads the same data you see in the Sunsynk app, which the inverter's WiFi or Ethernet data logger uploads to the Sunsynk cloud. Deye owners on the Solarman or Deye Cloud apps have similar community integrations.

It's the quickest way to see your figures in Home Assistant. The catch is the delay: the numbers are only as fresh as the dongle's last upload, and they stop when your internet or the manufacturer's servers do. That's fine for a dashboard, and a poor fit for anything that switches a pump or geyser on solar surplus.

Route 2

Build your own RS485 reader

Sunsynk and Deye hybrids speak Modbus RTU over RS485, and the South African community has done excellent work here. The two best-known projects are slipx06's ESPHome configurations (an ESP32 with an RS485 module) and kellerza's Sunsynk add-on for Home Assistant OS (a USB RS485 adapter on the Home Assistant machine, publishing over MQTT).

Practical notes if you go this way:

  • If your lithium battery talks to the inverter over the BMS485 port, connect your reader to the inverter's separate RS485/Modbus port.
  • Single-phase and three-phase inverters use different register maps. Use the configuration that matches your model.
  • Keep the electronics away from the inverter's metal housing and run a short Ethernet cable out to avoid interference.
  • Opening the inverter's wiring cover is a job for a registered electrician or accredited solar installer.

You get real-time data with no cloud in the way. In exchange, you source the parts, build the enclosure and power supply, and keep the firmware up to date yourself.

Route 3

A ready-made local gateway

If you want the local, real-time result without the build, a finished gateway does the RS485 side for you. Our own is the SI Gateway: it reads the inverter every 5 seconds, runs ESPHome underneath, and Home Assistant discovers it on your network as a native ESPHome device. No add-on, no API key and no cloud account.

Getting it into Home Assistant:

  1. Mount the gateway next to the inverter and connect it to the RS485 port with the supplied Ethernet cable.
  2. Power it up and join it to your WiFi from the SI app.
  3. In Home Assistant, open Settings → Devices & services. The gateway appears under ESPHome. Select Add.
  4. Load a free ready-made dashboard from our GitHub, or build your own from the sensors.

The automation logic lives on the gateway itself. It calculates a live ECM comfort score that SI Switch, SI Pool and SI Water read directly, so your loads keep running correctly even if Home Assistant is restarting or offline. Home Assistant is a window onto the system, not a single point of failure.

Which should you choose?

Match the route to the job

  • Just want to see your figures in Home Assistant? The cloud integration is enough.
  • Enjoy building things and want full control? Go DIY with ESPHome or the Sunsynk add-on.
  • Want real-time automations that work without the internet, without the build? Use a ready-made local gateway.

Questions

Common questions

Can Home Assistant read a Sunsynk inverter without the cloud?

Yes. Reading the inverter over its RS485 port, either with a DIY ESP32 or Raspberry Pi reader or a ready-made gateway, keeps the data on your own network with no cloud account.

Does the same method work for Deye inverters?

Yes. Sunsynk and Deye hybrid inverters use the same Modbus register maps, so the RS485 routes work on both. Single-phase and three-phase models need different configurations.

How often does the data update?

The cloud route updates when the dongle uploads, typically every 1 to 5 minutes. A local RS485 reader updates every few seconds; the SI Gateway reads every 5 seconds.

Do I need Home Assistant to use the SI Gateway?

No. The SI app and web portal work on their own, and the gateway's load control runs on the device. Home Assistant is optional.
